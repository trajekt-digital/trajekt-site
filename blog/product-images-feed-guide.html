Product images in feeds: size, format and quality requirements by channel

Every major Shopping channel has different image requirements. The minimum and recommended image specifications for Google Shopping, ChatGPT, Meta, TikTok, Pinterest, and Microsoft — and what to do when your images don't meet them.

By Trajekt·25 Jan 2026·January 28, 2026  ·  7 min read

Product images are the most visible element of any Shopping ad. Before a shopper reads your title or price, they've evaluated your image. Yet image quality is one of the most neglected aspects of feed management — primarily because most merchants assume their website images are good enough. Often they're not.

Google Shopping

Minimum: 100x100px (non-apparel), 250x250px (apparel). Recommended: 800x800px minimum for Shopping, 1200x1200px+ for Performance Max (large display formats). Avoid: watermarks, promotional text, borders. Apparel: show on model or mannequin, neutral background.

ChatGPT Shopping

Recommended: 800px+ square, HTTPS URLs only. Supports additional_image_urls (up to 4) — multiple angles improves completeness signal for ChatGPT's product evaluation.

Meta (Facebook & Instagram)

Minimum: 500x500px (enforced, rejected below this). Recommended: 1080x1080px square for Dynamic Product Ads. Up to 20 images per product via additional_image_link — more images give Meta's algorithm more creative options across ad formats.

TikTok

Recommended: 800x800px+ square for catalogue ads. The vertical opportunity: TikTok is a 9:16 vertical platform. If you have 1080x1920px vertical shots, include them — TikTok heavily rewards content that natively fits its format.

Pinterest

Recommended: 1000x1500px (2:3 vertical ratio). Pinterest is the platform where dimensions have the biggest commercial impact — a vertical image occupies twice the visual space of a square image in Pinterest's grid. More space = more attention = more clicks.

Microsoft/Bing Shopping

Minimum: 220x220px. Recommended: 800px+ — same as Google Shopping.

When your images don't meet requirements

  • Rule-based image selection: If products have multiple images at different orientations, Trajekt rules can select the most appropriate per channel — the vertical image for Pinterest, the high-res square for Google PMax.
  • Additional image links: Provide channels with more creative options without changing your primary image.
  • Background removal: AI-powered tools (remove.bg) can quickly produce clean-background shots for Google and Meta without a full photoshoot.

Image URL mapping: the hidden feed problem

One of the most overlooked product feed image issues isn't image quality — it's URL mapping. Many ecommerce platforms, particularly Shopify and WooCommerce, expose multiple image URLs per product: the original full-size image, several resized CDN variants, and sometimes thumbnail versions. A feed that automatically picks the first image URL from the API response may consistently return a 400×400px CDN thumbnail rather than the 1200×1200px original.

The fix is explicit URL mapping in your feed rules: map to the source image URL rather than any resized variant. In Shopify, this is the originalSrc field in the GraphQL API rather than any transformed URL. In WooCommerce, it's the src field at the image object level. Trajekt maps to the full-size source URL by default for this reason.

Multiple images: additional_image_link across channels

Google Shopping allows up to 10 additional images via the additional_image_link field. These appear in Shopping ad image carousels and in Performance Max creative assets. TikTok Shop supports a video_link field for product videos, which is a strong differentiator for visual products. Meta Dynamic Product Ads use multiple images in carousel format automatically from your catalogue.

The highest-value investment for multi-image feeds is lifestyle photography as secondary images alongside white-background hero shots. White-background images are required as primary images for most Shopping channels, but lifestyle images (product in use, product in context, product worn by model for apparel) are valuable as additional images for Performance Max and Meta's creative optimisation.

Automated image quality validation

Before your feed reaches any platform, it's worth validating image URLs automatically: check that each URL returns a 200 HTTP status (not a 404 or a redirect chain), that the content type is an image format (not HTML), and if possible that the image dimensions meet the channel minimum. Trajekt's pre-submission validation checks URL accessibility. For dimension validation, a lightweight image header check (reading just the first bytes of the image to extract dimensions without downloading the full file) is possible via tools like Pillow in Python if you're building a custom validation layer.
