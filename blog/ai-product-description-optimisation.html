How to optimise product descriptions for AI shopping assistants

Product descriptions written for Google Shopping won't work for ChatGPT and Perplexity. How AI shopping assistants evaluate product data differently, and how to write descriptions that perform in both channels.

By Trajekt·25 Mar 2026·June 18, 2026  ·  8 min read

Product descriptions have been written the same way for two decades: keyword-rich, benefit-led, often with marketing hyperbole. "Premium quality", "crafted with care", "the ultimate choice." This approach worked when the only reader was a keyword-matching algorithm. AI shopping assistants evaluate product data differently — and these descriptions fail them.

How Google Shopping reads descriptions

Google Shopping's description matching has historically been keyword-based: does your description contain terms matching the query? "Waterproof", "hiking", "lightweight" — present in your description, matched to queries containing those terms.

How AI assistants read descriptions

ChatGPT and Perplexity evaluate meaning. When a user asks "What's a good waterproof jacket for hiking in Wales in November?", they evaluate your product data for:

  • Factual accuracy: Does this product do what the description claims?
  • Specificity: Does it provide enough detail to evaluate fit for this query?
  • Context match: Do product characteristics match the implied use context (wet Welsh mountains, November)?

"Premium waterproof jacket perfect for all outdoor adventures" provides nothing for this evaluation. "Hardshell jacket with 20,000mm hydrostatic head and fully taped seams. Wind resistant to 40mph. Packable to 800ml. 310g." gives the AI everything it needs.

The five elements of an AI-ready description

  1. Factual specifications: Measurable, verifiable numbers — dimensions, weight, material composition, technical ratings
  2. Use case context: What is this for? Who uses it? In what circumstances?
  3. Material and construction: What it's made of and how it's made
  4. Compatibility and sizing: For anything dimensional, specific guidance dramatically improves AI matching
  5. No marketing language: "Revolutionary", "game-changing", "best-in-class" reduce AI trust in the description

Before and after

Before: "Elevate your outdoor adventures with our premium hiking jacket. Crafted with cutting-edge waterproof technology, this versatile jacket keeps you dry in any conditions."

After: "Hardshell hiking jacket with 20,000mm waterproof rating and fully taped seams. 3-layer construction. Weight: 310g. Pit zips for ventilation. Hood with wired peak. Suitable for sustained rainfall. Fits over mid-layer fleece. Machine washable."

The specific elements that AI descriptions need to include

The five elements of an AI-ready description aren't abstract principles — they translate to specific content requirements. Material: not just "cotton" but "100% organic cotton" if that's what it is. Dimensions: actual measurements, not "compact" or "large". Use case: specific contexts, not "perfect for any occasion". Technical specification: voltage, thread count, weight capacity, IP rating — whatever is genuinely relevant to the product category. Compatibility: what it works with, what it doesn't, which size or model it fits.

The test for a well-optimised description is whether a buyer who has never seen your product could make an informed purchase decision from the description alone, without seeing the product page. If the description requires the product page images or table to make sense, it's not self-sufficient enough for AI commerce contexts where it may appear without that visual support.

Batch optimisation vs product-by-product

For catalogues over a few hundred products, hand-writing descriptions is not viable. AI enrichment at batch scale requires a prompt that generalises well across product types while still generating specific, accurate output for each product. The key input variables in a batch enrichment prompt are: product title, existing description (even if poor quality), attributes (material, dimensions, specs), category, and brand. The richer this input data, the better the batch output — enrichment can't invent specifics that aren't in the source data.

In Trajekt's AI enrichment layer, the prompt runs once per product per change. If a product's title, description, or attributes haven't changed since the last run, the previously-generated description is served from cache. This means the ongoing cost of AI enrichment scales with how frequently your product data changes, not with catalogue size — for a stable catalogue, marginal ongoing cost is very low after the initial batch run.

What to do when AI descriptions underperform

If AI-enriched descriptions don't improve Shopping performance relative to your original descriptions, the diagnosis is usually one of three things. First: the original descriptions were already good, and enrichment didn't meaningfully improve them — this is actually a positive finding. Second: the enriched descriptions are accurate but use different language than your target customers search with — evaluate whether keyword-critical terms appear in titles (which you can control with rules) to compensate. Third: the enrichment prompt is generating generic rather than specific output — revisit the prompt design, add more required specificity, and run a spot-check across 20–30 products before concluding that enrichment doesn't work for your catalogue.
