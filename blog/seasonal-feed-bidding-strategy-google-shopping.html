Seasonal Bidding in Google Shopping: How to Use Your Feed to Stay Ahead

September 3, 2026  ·  9 min read

Google Shopping's seasonal demand patterns are highly predictable. Searches for winter coats rise from late September. Garden furniture peaks in March and April. Christmas gifting queries follow a consistent curve year on year. The merchants who win aren't those who react to these patterns — they're the ones who are ready before them, with correctly labelled products, appropriate bids, and optimised titles for seasonal search intent.

Your product feed is the mechanism that makes seasonal preparation scalable. Without feed automation, seasonal adjustments mean manually updating bids across hundreds of product groups before every season. With automated feed rules, the labels change on schedule and your campaign bids follow automatically.

The two-lag problem in seasonal Shopping

Seasonal Shopping strategy has two timing problems that compound each other. First, there's feed lag: changes in your product feed take 24–72 hours to propagate through Merchant Center and into Shopping results. If you update your seasonal labels on the first day of peak season, you miss the early peak. Second, there's Google's learning lag: Smart Bidding takes 1–2 weeks to accumulate enough data to optimise efficiently for a new seasonal pattern. If your campaign structure changes at the start of peak season, you spend the first two weeks of peak in Google's learning phase.

The solution is to lead seasonal changes by 2–3 weeks. Update seasonal labels and campaign bids before the peak, not when it starts. Your Smart Bidding algorithm has time to learn the seasonal demand pattern before the highest-traffic period arrives.

Building seasonal custom label rules

The cleanest seasonal label approach uses Shopify collections or WooCommerce categories as the signal — since these reflect your active merchandising decisions and are already updated by your buying team as seasons change.

# Seasonal labels driven by collections IF collection CONTAINS "Autumn Winter 2026" OR collection CONTAINS "AW26" THEN custom_label_1 = "aw26-current" IF collection CONTAINS "Spring Summer 2026" OR collection CONTAINS "SS26" AND NOT collection CONTAINS "Clearance" THEN custom_label_1 = "ss26-residual" IF collection CONTAINS "Clearance" OR collection CONTAINS "Sale" THEN custom_label_1 = "clearance" IF collection CONTAINS "New In" OR collection CONTAINS "New Arrivals" THEN custom_label_1 = "new-arrival" # Evergreen products not in a seasonal collection IF custom_label_1 IS EMPTY THEN custom_label_1 = "evergreen"

When your buying team moves products between collections as seasons change, the Trajekt rules automatically reassign labels on the next daily refresh. No manual label management required.

Seasonal title optimisation: think like a seasonal buyer

Search intent changes with the season. In September, someone searching for a jacket is likely starting autumn shopping. By December, they might be specifically looking for a Christmas gift. The words they use shift. A product titled "Barbour Quilted Jacket — Navy" performs well year-round. During peak gifting season, a rule that appends "— Gift Idea" to specific product types can improve visibility for gift-intent queries without permanently changing your Shopify product name.

# Seasonal title modifier — applied temporarily # (set date range or toggle manually) IF product_type CONTAINS "Fragrance" AND custom_label_1 = "aw26-current" THEN title = title + " — Gift Idea"

Peak season feed frequency

Your standard daily feed refresh is insufficient during peak trading periods. Black Friday week, Christmas week, and Boxing Day sales all involve rapid price and stock changes that a daily refresh captures too slowly. Switch to twice-daily refresh for these periods. In Trajekt, this is a single toggle change — your Merchant Center feed URL remains the same; only the generation frequency changes.

Twice-daily refresh during Black Friday means that if you drop a flash sale at 9am, Merchant Center has the updated price by 2pm rather than the following morning. The difference between showing a £99 price and showing a £69 flash sale price during a high-traffic shopping period can be significant in both CTR and conversion rate.

Post-season clearance: the often-missed feed opportunity

Most merchants adjust bids down on clearance products. Fewer adjust their feed strategy. Clearance titles benefit from explicit "sale" and discount framing in the description (not the title — Google Shopping prohibits promotional language in titles). Clearance products often have missing stock at certain sizes — using inventory-based exclusion rules prevents Shopping driving traffic to your size-20 clearance jacket when you only have size-8 left.

Marking clearance products with a dedicated custom label also enables a separate campaign or ad group with a higher target ROAS — since clearance margins are compressed, you need better conversion rates to be profitable, which means qualifying traffic more aggressively.
