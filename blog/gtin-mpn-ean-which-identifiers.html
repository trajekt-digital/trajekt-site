GTIN, MPN, EAN: which product identifiers does your feed actually need?

The complete breakdown of product identifiers for UK Shopping feeds. When GTINs are required, when MPNs are acceptable, how UK EAN-13 format works, and how to handle products without any barcode.

By Trajekt·1 Jan 2026·February 19, 2026  ·  7 min read

Three letters — GTIN, MPN, EAN — cause a disproportionate amount of confusion in product feed management. Here are practical answers for UK merchants.

What each identifier actually is

GTIN is the umbrella term for all barcode-based product identifiers. EAN, UPC, ISBN are all types of GTIN. In Google Shopping, submit the barcode number in the gtin field regardless of type.

EAN-13 is the 13-digit barcode format used on most UK and European products. UK EANs typically start with prefix 50.

UPC-A is the American 12-digit equivalent. You can convert UPC-A to EAN-13 by prepending a 0.

MPN (Manufacturer Part Number) is a manufacturer-assigned code — not a global identifier, just the code the manufacturer uses internally. Typically alphanumeric. Two manufacturers could assign the same MPN to different products.

When does Google Shopping require a GTIN?

  • Products you buy from manufacturers or distributors and resell: almost always have GTINs
  • Branded products you manufacture yourself: register GTINs with GS1 UK (from £119/year)
  • Handmade, custom, or one-of-a-kind products: no GTIN — use identifier_exists: no
  • Vintage or antique products: no GTIN — use identifier_exists: no

MPN as a fallback

If a product has no GTIN but has a manufacturer part number — common in automotive parts, electronics components — submit MPN + brand as the identifier combination. Google accepts this where MPN is the standard industry identifier. If you have an EAN, use it. MPN is a fallback, not a first choice.

The EAN checksum validation problem

EAN-13 barcodes include a check digit calculated from the first 12 digits. If your stored data has been corrupted — Excel treating the 13-digit number as scientific notation (5.05E+12), leading zeros stripped by a numeric database field — the checksum fails and Google rejects it as invalid.

How much does GTIN coverage actually matter?

Products with correct GTINs consistently show higher impression share and better CTR than equivalent products without GTINs. Google uses GTINs to match products to its product knowledge graph — a database of millions of products with associated shopping signals. Products matched to this graph get additional ranking signals that unidentified products don't access. Improving GTIN coverage from 60% to 95% typically yields a meaningful increase in total Google Shopping impressions.

Measuring GTIN coverage in your feed

Before fixing GTIN coverage, measure where you are. In Google Merchant Center Diagnostics, the "Missing value [GTIN]" warning tells you how many products are affected, and the Products tab lets you filter by this warning to see which specific SKUs are missing barcodes. For most retailers, the missing GTINs cluster in specific categories — own-label products (which legitimately don't need them), very old stock (where barcodes were never recorded digitally), or products from suppliers who don't provide barcode data in their catalogue exports.

The priority for GTIN improvement is branded products without GTINs — these are the items where Google's matching algorithm is actively disadvantaged and impression share is being left on the table. Own-brand products without GTINs set identifier_exists: no and move on. For branded products with missing GTINs, the most reliable source is your supplier data — many suppliers provide GTINs in their product data exports or on product packaging that can be scanned and added to your catalogue.

EAN-13 checksum: why invalid barcodes cause silent problems

EAN-13 barcodes include a check digit — the 13th digit is calculated from the first 12. Google's Merchant Center validates this checksum and silently ignores products with invalid GTINs rather than flagging them as disapproved. This means you can submit a GTIN, see no error in Diagnostics, but still lose the matching benefit because the barcode is invalid. The most common cause of invalid GTINs in feed data is manual data entry errors — someone typed a 12-digit barcode into a 13-digit field, or transposed two digits.

EAN-13 checksum validation is straightforward to implement in a rules engine: multiply alternating digits by 1 and 3, sum all 13 results, verify the total is divisible by 10. Trajekt validates EAN-13 checksums before submission and flags invalid GTINs for correction rather than passing them through to Merchant Center.

MPN as a complementary identifier

For products with a valid MPN (manufacturer part number) but no GTIN — common in automotive parts, industrial supplies, and some electronics — submitting the MPN alongside identifier_exists: no tells Google specifically what identifier type you have. Some Google Shopping categories give MPN-identified products preferential matching for category-specific query types, particularly automotive and electronics where MPNs are widely searched. GTIN is always preferred when available, but MPN is a meaningful fallback rather than no identifier at all.
