Microsoft Shopping: the overlooked channel UK retailers are ignoring

56 million UK monthly Bing users. Higher household income than Google. Lower CPCs. And free product listings. Here's the honest case for adding Microsoft Shopping to your channel mix.

By Trajekt·1 Apr 2026·April 2, 2026  ·  6 min read

A statistic most UK ecommerce managers should find uncomfortable: 56 million people in the UK use Bing every month, and the majority of UK online retailers are completely invisible to all of them. Not because Microsoft Shopping is difficult to set up — because it's not Google and gets deprioritised indefinitely.

Who the Bing audience actually is

The Bing stereotype is people who forgot to change their browser default. Reality: the audience skews toward professionals aged 35+, business owners, Windows laptop users, and Microsoft 365 users who encounter Bing through Edge and Windows. Approximately 40% of UK Bing users have household income above £75,000.

For premium products, B2B-adjacent consumer goods, home improvement, or anything where the buyer skews older and more affluent — this demographic is more valuable than Google's broader audience, not less.

The CPC advantage

Microsoft Shopping CPCs are typically 15–30% lower than Google Shopping for equivalent placements. Lower CPCs + higher-income audience = better cost per acquisition for the right categories. The merchants who discover this early capture a significant efficiency advantage.

Free listings: completely free traffic

Microsoft offers free product listings in the Bing Shopping tab — organic placement with zero ad spend. If you have a valid Microsoft Merchant Center feed, your products appear in free listings automatically. This alone often justifies the setup time.

The Google import shortcut

Microsoft Merchant Center has a built-in Google Import tool that can import your Google Merchant Center data directly. If you're running Google Shopping via Trajekt, the fastest path to Microsoft Shopping:

  1. Create a Microsoft Advertising account (free)
  2. Set up Microsoft Merchant Center
  3. Use the Google Import tool with your Google Merchant Center feed URL
  4. Create a Shopping campaign in Microsoft Advertising

The whole process can take under an hour for a merchant already running Google Shopping.

What the Google import actually copies — and what it misses

Microsoft's Google import is genuinely useful for initial setup: it pulls your Google Merchant Center feed, campaigns, ad groups, and keywords in one step. For most merchants this gets you to a working Bing Shopping setup within an hour. But the import is a snapshot — it doesn't stay synchronised. Price changes, new products, and availability updates in your Google feed don't flow automatically to Microsoft.

The production-ready approach is a dedicated Microsoft Shopping feed URL pointing directly to your feed management tool, separate from any Google import. This means Microsoft Shopping always reflects your latest product data on its own schedule — not Google's schedule delayed by whatever Microsoft's import cycle is.

Microsoft-specific feed differences

Microsoft Merchant Center accepts feeds in a format very similar to Google Shopping XML, but with some differences worth knowing. The condition field uses slightly different values. The destination field controls which Microsoft ad formats your products appear in. Microsoft also has its own product category taxonomy, though it accepts Google's taxonomy IDs as a fallback — meaning a Google Shopping XML feed submitted to Microsoft will process without category remapping in most cases.

The more meaningful difference is Microsoft's free listings program. Like Google's free Shopping tab, Microsoft offers free product listings to merchants with verified Merchant Center accounts. These appear in Bing Shopping results at no cost and require no additional configuration beyond having an active feed and a verified domain.

Realistic expectations for UK Microsoft Shopping

The honest picture: Microsoft Shopping will not match Google Shopping in volume for most UK merchants. The audience is smaller and the shopping query volume is lower. But lower competition frequently produces lower CPCs, and the Bing demographic (older, more affluent, often higher average order value) converts well for certain categories — particularly B2C electronics, home, and premium fashion. Evaluate it as a supplementary channel at modest budget allocation rather than a primary one, and let 60 days of data tell you whether it earns more spend.
