Performance Max vs Standard Shopping: how your feed affects both

Your Google Shopping feed powers both Standard Shopping and Performance Max. But the feed quality signals that matter most differ. Here's what to optimise for each campaign type.

By Trajekt·28 Apr 2026·April 30, 2026  ·  8 min read

Performance Max (PMax) campaigns generate heated debate in PPC. But one thing is rarely discussed: how the same product feed behaves differently in PMax versus Standard Shopping, and what to optimise accordingly.

What they share

Both campaign types draw from the same product data in Google Merchant Center. There's no separate PMax feed — your Merchant Center catalogue powers both simultaneously. Improvements to your feed benefit both immediately.

Where they differ: how Google uses your data

Standard Shopping uses your product title, description, and google_product_category primarily for keyword-based query matching. Title optimisation — adding brand, attributes, size, colour — has a direct, measurable impact on impression share.

Performance Max blends feed data with audience intelligence, creative assets, and cross-channel inventory. PMax generates ad creative automatically across Search, Shopping, YouTube, Display, Discover, and Gmail.

For PMax specifically: image quality matters more (images appear at large display sizes in YouTube and Display formats), description quality matters more (PMax uses descriptions for ad copy across multiple formats), and title keyword density matters less.

Custom labels: more important in PMax

In Standard Shopping, custom labels enable bid segmentation. In PMax, they enable asset group segmentation. Create separate PMax asset groups for different product segments, each with tailored creative assets and bidding. A "hero products" label, a "seasonal" label, and a "clearance" label let you run completely different PMax strategies for each.

Product type: the PMax grouping field

Your product_type field is particularly valuable for PMax. Clean, hierarchical product_type values (Mens > Jackets > Waxed Jackets) give PMax useful category context for its machine learning. Inconsistent values deprive PMax of one of its most useful grouping signals.

If PMax is treating all your products as one undifferentiated pool, the most likely cause is missing or inconsistent product_type and custom_label data in your feed.

PMax feed optimisation checklist

  • ✅ Titles: factual, descriptive, include brand and key attributes
  • ✅ Descriptions: minimum 150 words, factual, include key specs and use cases
  • ✅ Images: high quality, min 1200px wide for PMax Display placements
  • ✅ Custom labels: segmented by margin tier, seasonality, bestseller status
  • ✅ Product type: clean hierarchy, consistent across catalogue
  • ✅ GTINs: complete coverage for branded products

Asset group strategy and feed quality

Performance Max campaign performance is split across asset groups, and each asset group can be targeted to a specific product subset via listing groups (which filter by feed attributes like custom_label or brand). This means your feed's custom labels and google_product_category values directly control which products appear in which asset groups — and therefore which creative assets, audiences, and bidding strategies are applied to them.

A common PMax mistake is running all products in a single asset group with generic creative assets. This works adequately but misses the optimisation opportunity. A better structure: separate asset groups for top categories or margin tiers, each with category-specific creative assets (images, headlines, descriptions) and the corresponding feed filter. The creative relevance improves quality scores and the bidding signal improves as Google learns which assets perform for which product groups.

Feed signals that Performance Max uses differently from Standard Shopping

Standard Shopping campaigns use your feed primarily for product matching and title/description keyword relevance. Performance Max uses your feed for all of that plus creative generation: Google's AI can generate image assets, headline combinations, and descriptions from your product data. This means feed quality affects not just whether your product appears, but how it's presented across Search, Display, YouTube, Gmail, and Discover placements.

The feed fields that matter most for PMax's creative layer beyond the Standard Shopping basics: lifestyle_image_link (used for display and YouTube creatives), additional_image_link (carousel formats), and description quality (used to generate headline and description asset combinations). Products with rich, accurate descriptions and multiple high-quality images give PMax's AI more to work with — and PMax's learning period is shorter as a result.

Audience signals and your product catalogue

PMax allows you to add audience signals — lists of customer data, remarketing audiences, or in-market segments — to guide Google's targeting. Feed quality interacts with audience signals in a specific way: Google cross-references your audience signals with product interest data inferred from your feed categories and descriptions. A product in the correct google_product_category will be shown to users Google identifies as interested in that category; a miscategorised product misses this targeting signal entirely.

For merchants with established Shopping history, the single highest-impact action before launching a PMax campaign is a feed quality audit: correct categories, complete GTINs, optimised titles, and custom labels configured. Google's ML picks up from wherever your data quality is at the start of the learning period — poor quality data means a longer learning period and weaker initial performance.
