Google Vehicle Ads: the complete UK setup guide (2026)

Everything a UK car, van or leisure-vehicle dealer needs to launch Google Vehicle Ads and keep them approved — the accounts, the feed, the UK-specific details that trip dealers up, and how to feed it all straight from your DMS.

By Trajekt · 18 August 2026 · 11 min read

Google Vehicle Ads put your actual stock — this specific car, at this price, with these photos — in front of people searching Google for their next vehicle. Not a generic "used cars near you" banner: the exact unit on your forecourt, with a picture, price and mileage, linking straight to its page. For a UK dealer it's one of the highest-intent ad formats there is, because the person clicking has already told Google what they want.

Most guides to this are American, out of date, or gloss over the parts that actually get UK feeds rejected. This one is written for the UK market in 2026: the accounts you need, the exact data Google wants, the VIN/GDPR and store_code details that make or break approval, why feeds get disapproved and how to fix each cause, and the shortest path to keeping it all live — straight from your dealer system.

What Google Vehicle Ads actually are

Vehicle Ads (Google sometimes calls them vehicle listing ads) are a Shopping-style format built for dealers. Instead of the standard product-shopping card, they show vehicle-specific detail — make, model, year, mileage, price — pulled from a feed of your inventory. They appear on Google Search and the Shopping tab when someone searches for something your stock matches, like "used Ford Kuga under £18,000" or "Bailey Unicorn caravan for sale".

They're available to UK dealerships and run through Performance Max campaigns now, not the old Smart Shopping setup. Performance Max means Google spreads your vehicle inventory across Search, Shopping, Maps, YouTube and the Display network automatically, optimising towards the goal you set (usually leads or visits). You supply the inventory and the goal; Google handles the placement.

The three accounts you need (and how they connect)

This is where a lot of dealers get stuck, because Vehicle Ads sit across three Google products that all have to be linked correctly:

  • Google Merchant Center — where your vehicle feed lives. You submit your inventory here and Google checks it against its policies.
  • Google Ads — where the Performance Max campaign runs and your budget sits. It's linked to Merchant Center so it can advertise the approved inventory.
  • Google Business Profile — your verified dealership location(s). This is what ties an online ad to your physical forecourt, and it's linked to Merchant Center too.

The critical join between them is the store_code: a code on each Business Profile location that must appear, spelled identically, in your feed. Get this wrong and vehicles either won't serve or won't attribute to the right branch. Multi-site dealer groups need one store_code per location, and every vehicle's feed row has to carry the code for the site it's physically at.

The feed: what data Google needs

The feed is the whole game. Google can only advertise what you describe accurately, and it will disapprove anything that looks incomplete or inconsistent. Here are the fields, split into what's required and what's strongly recommended.

priceRequiredIn GBP, VAT-inclusive, and it must match the price on the landing page exactly.
link, image_linkRequiredThe vehicle's own page, and a primary image URL (HTTPS).
make, model, yearRequiredThe core matching signals — keep them clean and consistent.

The UK-specific details that get feeds approved

This is the part the American guides skip, and it's where UK dealers lose the most time:

VIN is optional — and you probably shouldn't send it. Under UK GDPR a VIN can be personal data (it links to a specific vehicle and, through the DVLA, potentially a person). Google makes VIN optional for the UK precisely for this reason. Submit your internal stock number as the identifier instead; it does the same job for matching without the data-protection headache.

Miles, not kilometres. Obvious, but automated feed tools that default to metric quietly ship "34500 km" and tank your relevance. Mileage should read in miles.

VAT-inclusive prices. The feed price must be what the customer sees — inc VAT — and it must match the landing page to the penny. Ex-VAT prices, or a feed that lags a price change on the website, are the single most common cause of disapprovals.

store_code discipline. One per branch, identical in the Business Profile and the feed. For a group, this is the field that decides whether a vehicle is advertised as being at Leeds or at Hull.

Landing pages: the rules that trip dealers up

Google crawls the page each vehicle links to and checks it against the feed. Two things have to be true or the vehicle is disapproved: the price on the page must match the feed, and the vehicle's id must be present on the page. If your website and your feed are updated on different schedules, they drift apart and Google rejects the mismatch. (This landing-page identity is also what makes Meta retargeting work — we cover the ID side of it in this post on matching the vehicle ID to your URL.)

Images

Use real photos of the actual vehicle, on a clean background, no promotional overlays or watermarks, HTTPS, and ideally 1200×900 or larger. Lead with a clear exterior three-quarter shot; add interior and detail shots as additional images. Google penalises stock/manufacturer imagery and duplicate photos across different vehicles, so genuine photography per unit pays off.

What it costs

Vehicle Ads run on your Google Ads budget as a Performance Max campaign, so you set the spend. As a rough UK starting point, a single-site dealer might begin around £15–£30/day and scale once cost-per-enquiry is proven; larger groups run substantially more. The right number isn't a guess — run it for 60–90 days, measure cost per genuine enquiry (form completion or call), and let that set your budget. Because the intent is high, vehicle ads often out-perform generic search for dealers, but only if the feed is complete enough for Google to match well.

Getting more from the same budget

Once you're approved and running, the wins come from feeding Google better signals:

Bid by margin. Not every vehicle is worth the same to you. If your feed carries margin data (retail vs cost), you can label stock with custom labels and let Performance Max lean into the profitable units. Most dealers can't do this because their website never had the cost figure — but if you feed straight from your DMS, it's right there. (More on that in why a direct feed beats the website chain.)

Segment by what sells. Custom labels let you group stock by age in days, price band, body style or campaign priority, then structure Performance Max around them.

Refresh often. Twice-daily updates keep prices accurate and sold stock out — both of which protect approval and stop you paying for clicks on vehicles you can't sell.

Caravans, motorhomes and leisure vehicles

Google Vehicle Ads aren't just for cars. Caravans, motorhomes, campervans and other towables all qualify, and it's an underserved space for UK leisure-vehicle dealers who are still paying rising fees to classified portals. The feed works the same way — make, model, year, price, images — with body style used to describe the vehicle type. Frequently asked questions

Are Google Vehicle Ads available in the UK? Yes. They run to UK dealerships through Performance Max, with UK handling for VIN (optional), mileage in miles, GBP pricing and store_code mapping to your Google Business Profile.

Do I need a VIN? No — in the UK it's optional and usually best left out (it's personal data). Use your internal stock number as the identifier.

Why do my vehicles keep getting disapproved? Almost always a price mismatch between feed and landing page, a missing required field, or a store_code that doesn't match your Business Profile. The table above covers the fixes.

How often should the feed update? At least daily, ideally twice a day, so prices stay accurate and sold vehicles drop out before you pay for clicks on them.

Can I advertise caravans and motorhomes? Yes — all vehicle types qualify, including caravans, motorhomes and campervans.

Do I have to build the feed myself? No. Trajekt reads your inventory and produces the feed automatically, with the UK-specific details handled for you.
