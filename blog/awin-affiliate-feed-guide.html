Awin affiliate feed setup: the complete guide for UK merchants

Everything UK merchants need to know to set up their Awin product feed correctly — field names, CSV format, update frequency, and how Awin publishers actually use your feed.

By Trajekt·18 Mar 2026·March 26, 2026  ·  7 min read

Awin connects 225,000+ publishers with thousands of UK merchants. For most UK ecommerce businesses, affiliate marketing via Awin is the highest-ROAS channel in the mix — once set up correctly. The product feed is the foundation of your programme.

Awin CSV field names you actually need

Awin uses specific field names that differ from Google Shopping:

  • aw_product_id — unique product identifier (not 'id' or 'product_id')
  • product_name — product title
  • merchant_image_url — primary product image URL
  • aw_deep_link — product URL (Awin adds tracking parameters)
  • search_price — current selling price (numeric, no currency symbol)
  • display_price — formatted price shown to shoppers (e.g. "£29.99")
  • in_stock — 1 for in stock, 0 for out of stock
  • brand_name — brand/manufacturer
  • ean — EAN barcode for price comparison matching
  • colour — product colour (note: UK spelling required)

Who uses your Awin feed and how

Price comparison publishers use your search_price, ean, and aw_deep_link to list your products against competitors. Accurate EAN matching is essential.

Cashback sites (TopCashback, Quidco) use your product_name, merchant_image_url, and search_price in their own listings. Image quality and accurate pricing directly affects how your products are presented.

Content publishers pull product data via Awin's datafeeds API to populate affiliate links in their content automatically.

Update frequency on Awin

Daily updates are the minimum. Awin publishers who use your feed for price comparison listings show your products 24/7. If your feed price is £10 higher than actual (because you ran a flash sale the feed didn't capture), visitors from comparison sites see a different price — creating complaints and Awin policy issues.

Common Awin feed problems

  • Using Google field names: image_link instead of merchant_image_url, id instead of aw_product_id. Awin won't import if field names don't match their spec.
  • Missing EAN: Without EANs, your products don't appear in comparison publisher listings.
  • Missing in_stock: 0: Publishers continue promoting out-of-stock products if not updated.

The most common Awin feed rejection causes

Awin reviews feeds before activating them in their network — a process that can take 3–5 business days for a new feed. The most common rejection causes are: missing required fields (product URL and image URL are both required and frequently missing from exports that were built for Google Shopping without Awin-specific mapping), price format issues (Awin expects prices without currency symbols — "24.99" not "£24.99"), and description HTML (Awin accepts some HTML in descriptions but rejects certain tags).

After initial activation, ongoing disapprovals in Awin typically come from the same source as Google Shopping disapprovals: price changes not reflected in the feed, out-of-stock products still showing as available, and broken product URLs. The same daily refresh cadence that keeps your Google feed accurate keeps your Awin feed accurate.

How Awin publishers actually use your feed

Understanding how your feed gets used helps you optimise it. Price comparison publishers like PriceRunner and Shopzilla pull your Awin feed to list your products alongside competitors — accurate pricing and EANs for product matching are critical here. Cashback publishers like TopCashback and Quidco use your feed to display your products to deal-seeking shoppers — commission rate and product images matter most. Content publishers (bloggers, review sites) use your feed to generate affiliate links — title accuracy and product description quality affect which products get featured.

Most Awin publishers cache feed data rather than pulling live — which means a price on an affiliate site can lag your actual price by hours or even days on some publishers. This is a known limitation of the affiliate model. The practical mitigation is ensuring your feed is always accurate so that the cached version is as close to current as possible, and setting your promotional periods far enough in advance that publishers' caches have time to update.

Awin Deep Link Generator and your feed

For publishers who manually link to specific products rather than using feed data, Awin's Deep Link Generator creates tracked affiliate URLs from any product page URL on your site. These don't depend on your feed — they work from your website directly. But publishers using feed-based tools (automated product widgets, price comparison listings) are entirely dependent on your feed data quality. The split between feed-based and manual publisher usage varies by category and publisher type, but for most retail Awin programmes, feed quality affects the majority of affiliate revenue.
