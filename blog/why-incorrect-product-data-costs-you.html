Feed Strategy

Why 60% of UK shoppers abandon after encountering incorrect product data

Research shows 60% of UK shoppers felt misled after finding incorrect product data online. This article examines what stale feed data costs ecommerce businesses in revenue, ad spend, and brand trust.

By Trajekt·5 May 2026·May 7, 2026  ·  6 min read

A number that should be on every UK ecommerce manager's dashboard: 60% of UK shoppers have felt misled after encountering incorrect product information online. This isn't about deliberate deception — it's about price mismatches, out-of-stock products showing as available, wrong colours in ads. All of it comes back to one root cause: a product feed that hasn't been updated.

What incorrect data looks like in practice

  • Clicking a Shopping ad showing a sale price, arriving to find the sale has ended
  • A product showing as in stock on Google Shopping, then being told it's out of stock on the website
  • Ordering a product described as 'available in black' and receiving the only available colour — navy
  • Clicking an ad for a specific size, finding the size selector but discovering that size is unavailable

Each of these is a feed data problem. The ad showed data that was accurate when the feed last updated — but the feed hadn't refreshed in 24, 48, or 72 hours.

The cost per incident

Consider the economics of a single price mismatch on a high-traffic product: 200 clicks per day at £0.60 CPC = £120/day. If the advertised price was £10 lower than actual, a meaningful portion bounce immediately. Google's landing page quality signals penalise this, reducing impression share. Merchant Center flags the mismatch within 24–48 hours and may suspend the product.

The highest-risk scenarios for data divergence

  • Flash sales: Your feed shows full price while a 25%-off sale is live, or shows sale price after it's ended
  • Fast-moving stock: Products that sell from 50 units to 0 between feed updates
  • Manual price changes: Updated on website but the feed hasn't refreshed

The Merchant Center enforcement cycle

Google actively crawls your product landing pages and compares them to your feed data. When it finds mismatches: the product receives a price mismatch flag, Google may automatically pause it, and repeated or widespread mismatches can escalate to account-level warnings. Regular, automated feed updates are the only reliable protection.

Beyond Google: the cross-channel data accuracy problem

Price mismatches don't just affect Google Shopping. If your Meta DPAs show expired sale prices, your TikTok Shop shows out-of-stock products as available, or your Awin publishers are promoting old pricing — each creates the same experience. A centralised feed management approach with simultaneous multi-channel updates eliminates the "which channel is showing stale data?" problem entirely.

Data divergence across multiple channels

The data accuracy problem compounds as you add channels. A price change in your Shopify store that your feed tool picks up and pushes to Google Shopping might not reach your Meta catalogue until the following day's refresh, creating a 24-hour window where the same product shows at different prices on different platforms. With ChatGPT Shopping, the lag can be longer still — OpenAI's processing pipeline adds additional time between SFTP delivery and index update.

The mitigation is uniform refresh frequency across all channels from a single source feed. When all channels pull from the same feed run on the same schedule, data divergence between platforms is minimised. The only remaining risk is the lag between your store updating (a sale price goes live at 9am) and your feed refreshing (scheduled for 2am). Twice-daily refreshes and manual re-run capability reduce this to a manageable window.

The Awin-specific data accuracy risk

Affiliate channels introduce a specific data accuracy risk: third-party publishers who cache your feed data on their own servers. A cashback site that pulls your Awin feed once a day may show your prices from yesterday. A price comparison site that refreshes weekly can show prices from last week. When your promotional period ends and regular pricing resumes, some publishers will still be showing the sale price for days or weeks, effectively extending your discount beyond its intended window and reducing margin.

The direct mitigation is ensuring your Awin feed is accurate and updated promptly, but you can't control publisher refresh rates. The indirect mitigation is building discount structures that are sustainable for a longer effective window — pricing promotions that still make business sense if they're live on some publishers for 3–7 days longer than intended.

Building a data accuracy monitoring habit

The merchants who maintain consistently accurate product data treat it as an operational discipline rather than a one-time setup task. Practical habits that catch data accuracy issues before they become costly: a weekly spot-check of 10–20 products from your feed against your website (price, availability, URL resolves); a Merchant Center email alert configured for any new disapproval spike; and a feed run notification so you know immediately if a scheduled run fails. The cumulative cost of undetected data inaccuracy across Shopping, Awin, and Meta is typically far larger than the time investment in these monitoring habits.
