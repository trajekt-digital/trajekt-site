Kelkoo vs Google Shopping: where UK price-conscious shoppers actually are

When does Kelkoo outperform Google Shopping? Which product categories work best on price comparison engines? And how to run both channels without doubling your feed management workload.

By Trajekt·15 Jan 2026·January 22, 2026  ·  6 min read

Kelkoo and Google Shopping both show your products to UK shoppers — but they reach different buyers at different stages with different intent. Understanding the difference can significantly improve your channel mix ROI.

The fundamental difference in buyer intent

Google Shopping reaches buyers at various stages: research, comparison, ready-to-buy. The intent signal is strong but mixed.

Kelkoo reaches shoppers who are already price-comparing. A Kelkoo user has typically decided what they want and is finding the cheapest legitimate source. Conversion intent is high — but if you're not price-competitive, you don't win.

Which categories perform better on Kelkoo

Kelkoo works best for products where: the product is identifiable by model number or EAN, price comparison is the primary decision factor (electronics, appliances), and your price is competitive — within 5–10% of market rate.

Doesn't work well for: fashion, luxury goods, handmade or unique products, products with complex configuration.

The EAN requirement

Kelkoo's price comparison engine matches products by EAN barcode. Without an accurate EAN, your product either doesn't appear in comparison listings or appears next to a different product. EAN accuracy is critical on Kelkoo in a way it's important-but-optional on some other channels.

CPCs: Kelkoo vs Google Shopping

Kelkoo typically operates at lower CPCs than Google Shopping — often 10–25p vs 50p–£1.50+ for electronics. The trade-off: lower CPC but typically lower conversion rate (Kelkoo traffic is more price-sensitive). Net result for price-competitive merchants: similar or better CPA on Kelkoo for eligible categories.

How to generate both feeds from one source

The practical objection to running Kelkoo alongside Google Shopping is the management overhead — maintaining two separate feeds in two separate formats with two separate upload processes. This is the problem a feed management tool solves. Trajekt connects to your Shopify or WooCommerce store once and generates both Google Shopping XML and Kelkoo CSV from the same product data on the same daily refresh cycle.

The only Kelkoo-specific configuration required is field mapping: Kelkoo uses different field names from Google (their category field is called "category" not "google_product_category", their brand field maps differently). A one-time rules setup handles this — once configured, both feeds stay in sync automatically.

Kelkoo-specific optimisations worth making

Because Kelkoo's matching algorithm is more heavily weighted toward exact product matching (via EAN/GTIN) than Google's, the highest-impact optimisation for Kelkoo is barcode completeness. A product with a valid EAN-13 on Kelkoo will be matched to existing product pages, price comparisons, and related merchant listings far more reliably than a product without one.

Title structure matters less on Kelkoo than on Google (there's no query matching based on title keywords — Kelkoo matches your EAN to a product record), but descriptions should still be clean and specific. Category mapping to Kelkoo's own taxonomy improves placement in their category browse pages.

When Kelkoo makes financial sense

Kelkoo operates on a cost-per-click model, so the question is always whether Kelkoo CPCs convert well enough to justify the spend versus your Google Shopping CPCs. The category and brand data from Kelkoo's reporting gives you enough to make this evaluation within the first 30–60 days. Run both, evaluate the comparative ROAS at category level, and let the data decide which deserves more budget.
