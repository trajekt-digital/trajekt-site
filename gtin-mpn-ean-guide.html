FAQ

Frequently asked questions

A GTIN (Global Trade Item Number) is a unique product identifier — essentially a structured barcode number. GTINs encompass several different formats: EAN-13 (most common in UK/Europe, 13 digits), UPC-A (US/Canada, 12 digits), ISBN (books, 10 or 13 digits), ISSN (periodicals, 8 digits), ITF-14 (shipping containers, 14 digits), and EAN-8 (small products, 8 digits). In UK ecommerce, EAN-13 is by far the most common.
These are all types of product identifiers that fall under the GTIN umbrella: EAN (European Article Number, 13 digits) is standard in the UK and Europe. UPC (Universal Product Code, 12 digits) is standard in North America. ISBN (International Standard Book Number) identifies books. ISSN identifies periodicals. All of these can be submitted in the gtin field in Google Shopping — Google accepts any valid GTIN format.
If your products have barcodes from a manufacturer (i.e. you're reselling branded goods), you should always include the GTIN — it significantly improves Google Shopping match quality and impression share. If you manufacture your own products, you may have registered GTINs via GS1 UK, or your products may genuinely have no GTIN. Custom, handmade, or vintage items typically have no GTIN.
identifier_exists: no tells Google that your product doesn't have a GTIN or MPN — for example, custom-made jewellery, vintage clothing, or craft products. Without this field, Google assumes your product should have a GTIN and may disapprove it when one isn't provided. Only set identifier_exists: no for products that genuinely have no manufacturer identifier.
MPN (Manufacturer Part Number) is the product code assigned by the manufacturer — different from the consumer-facing barcode. MPNs are typically alphanumeric (e.g. BC1408NY2) and specific to the manufacturer's product line. If your product has no GTIN but does have an MPN (common in automotive parts, electronics components, and B2B products), submit the MPN along with brand as an alternative identifier.

UK businesses register for GTINs through GS1 UK (gs1uk.org). GS1 assigns you a company prefix (the first 7-9 digits) and you then create unique product numbers within that prefix. GS1 UK membership starts from £119/year for small businesses and includes a company prefix for up to 10 products. Each additional product range increases the membership level.
If you submit a GTIN that doesn't pass the checksum algorithm, Google will reject it with a "Invalid GTIN" error. If you submit a GTIN that exists in Google's database but belongs to a different product (wrong barcode), Google may match your listing to the wrong product in its Shopping catalogue, showing incorrect information. Always verify your barcodes are accurate.

Products with correct GTINs typically see: better match quality (more relevant query triggering), improved Quality Score equivalents, higher impression share, eligibility for rich product cards in Google Shopping, and inclusion in Google's product knowledge graph for popular products. Products without GTINs (where one should exist) have reduced visibility.
